The Origin Story
“I lost ₹23,000 of my hard-earned savings to an OLX scam. What shook me most was how convincing, professional, and ordinary everything looked right up until the money was gone.”

Connecting with dozens of victims across Indian subreddits (r/delhi, r/bangalore, r/mumbai), I saw the same heartbreaking cycle repeating every single day: copied device photos, impossible bargain prices, spoofed defense IDs, and urgent pressure to pay advance deposits via UPI.

With generative AI and automation rising, deceptive sellers operate nationwide syndicates while everyday consumers are forced to guess who to trust. Why should the buyer bear the entire burden of finding the truth? That question inspired TrustLens.

The 6 Core Findings

What the Research Proves

1. Low Price Is Just a Hint

127 listings were 35% below normal. While extreme discounts match victim complaints, genuine reasons (scratched screens, urgent moves) also cause price drops. Price alone is never proof.

2. Titles Are Mass-Copied

666 listings (22.3%) had word-for-word identical titles. More than 52% used aggressive condition claims like "sealed pack" or "mint condition" to fabricate urgency.

3. Photos Travel Across India

164 exact duplicate image pairs were detected. 78% were posted in different cities and states, revealing extensive inter-city media reuse across disparate accounts.

4. Packaging OCR Catches Lies

By extracting text from device boxes and screens, TrustLens found 68 cross-modal contradictions, including a listing advertising an iPhone 13 while the box showed an iPhone 13 Mini.

5. AI Detectors Need Consensus

Testing two independent vision models revealed a 24.5% disagreement rate. Platforms relying on a single AI detector risk severe false accusations against honest sellers.

6. Corroborated Multi-Signal Queue

Rather than guessing with linear risk scores, TrustLens prioritized 213 candidate pairs where 2, 3, or 4 independent forensic signals agreed. 5 pairs met on 4 separate forensic layers.

Ethical AI & Boundaries

What TrustLens Can — and Cannot — Prove

A core contribution of this research is establishing strict scientific guardrails. We measure observable physical and mathematical evidence, not criminal intent.

Signal Type What TrustLens CAN Establish What TrustLens CANNOT Establish Alone
Price Anomaly Exact mathematical distance from category median. Seller malice, broken hardware, or distress sale intent.
Binary Image Hash Byte-for-byte SHA-256 equality between files. Image copyright ownership or proof of theft.
DINOv2 Embeddings High semantic visual similarity in pose and lighting. Proof that two photos depict the same physical device.
Packaging OCR Legible text visible on retail boxes and phone screens. Physical possession of the depicted item.
AI Detectors Model-level activations indicating synthetic artifacts. Definitive proof of generative AI synthesis without review.
Isolation Forest Multivariate statistical distance from the population. A "fraud" classification or scam prediction.
The Core Conclusion

AI does not need to guess “scam” to protect people.

Modern marketplaces don't need another black-box AI score falsely accusing sellers. What platforms need is an evidence-first investigation layer: detecting photo reuse across cities, verifying packaging OCR against titles, flagging off-platform contact redirection, and grouping coordinated account rings.

Trust shouldn't begin with a warning after a victim sends money. It must begin with investigating the listing before it reaches the buyer.
